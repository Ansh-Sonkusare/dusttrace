[2:20 · 40 s] Here's DustTrace on one Mumbai street. One: the forecast, built from CPCB stations and weather, flags tomorrow's spike. Two: our plume model follows the wind from the site and finds who's downwind, here a school, and our own sensor node at the school measures what the children actually breathe. Three, our first unique element: the site gets its Safe-Work Window, so demolition moves to the afternoon and the mist cannon starts before the spike. Four: the site's own sensor is checked against the station and its neighbours, and the satellite finds the site next door that has no sensor. Five, our second unique element. Every site already has to show an LED board with its live dust readings. We add two things to it: a Dust Score out of 100 that tells passers-by how well this site controls its dust, and a QR code. A resident who sees dust scans it, snaps a photo, and the report goes straight to that site's record. Those reports and scores feed the officer's ranked inspection list for the next morning. Most of this runs on hardware Mumbai already has: the board, the site sensor and the stations. Where there is no sensor at all, we add our own low-cost nodes, which is next.

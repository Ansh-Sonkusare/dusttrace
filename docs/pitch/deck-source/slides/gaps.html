Where existing solutions fall short

Four gaps between the sensors and enforcement

Untrusted data

Each sensor is owned, placed and maintained by the developer it monitors, and nobody checks it.

Blind spots

Homes, schools and many sites have no sensor at all; unregistered demolition never will.

Nothing prevents

Sprinklers and inspections start after AQI turns bad. No one tells a site when not to demolish.

No attribution

A CAAQMS station reads a whole neighbourhood. It cannot say which of dozens of upwind sites caused a spike.

Sources: Karmactive (Oct 2025); Free Press Journal (Nov 2025)

[1:25 · 30 s] So where does it break? Four gaps. One: untrusted data. The sensor is owned, placed and maintained by the very developer it's supposed to monitor. Two: blind spots. The homes and schools around sites have no sensor at all, and unregistered demolitions never will. Three, and most important: nothing prevents. Sprinklers and inspections start after the air turns bad, and no one tells a site when not to demolish. Four: no attribution. A monitoring station reads a whole neighbourhood. It can't tell you which of dozens of sites upwind caused the spike.

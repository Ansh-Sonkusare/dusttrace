[0:50 · 35 s] Construction dust is Mumbai's leading air-pollution source. That's not our claim; it's from BMC's own Air Pollution Mitigation Plan. The city has about twelve hundred active construction sites. After a High Court order, every one of them has to run an air-quality sensor. But by October 2025, only 45 percent had one. In November, a 95-ward inspection found 117 sensors switched off or inactive, and BMC issued 62 stop-work notices in a single drive. Since June 2025, every one of those sensors has to stream to BMC every thirty seconds. So Mumbai has the IoT. What it can't do is trust it, see past it, or use it to prevent a spike. BACKUP: If a judge asks about the share of construction: estimates vary by method. SAFAR (2022) put construction at about 8% of city particulate matter, with Metro works alone at 3.2% of suspended dust; NEERI found construction plus natural dust at 27% of PM10 at Dharavi. Hyperlocally, near active sites, it dominates. Before the zonal: check whether BMC has published newer 2026 compliance figures.

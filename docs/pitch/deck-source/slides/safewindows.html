How it's computed, per site, per hour

  • Wind, mixing height and rain for the next 48 h
  • Who is downwind: schools, hospitals, homes
  • Tasks: demolition, excavation, cutting, loading

Why it works

Same work, better hour. No new equipment, no work lost, just less dust landing on people.

Measured in Round 2 as person-exposure avoided.

[3:35 · 35 s] Our first unique element: Safe-Work Windows. The same demolition does very different harm depending on when it happens. On a winter morning, the air near the ground is shallow and still, and if the wind is blowing toward a school, that dust lands on children. By afternoon, the sea breeze and a deeper mixing layer spread it out. So for every site, for every hour of the next two days, we combine the wind forecast, mixing height, rain and who is downwind, and output Go, Caution or Avoid for the dustiest tasks. The site does the same work, just at a better hour. No new equipment, no work lost. This example is illustrative. In Round 2 we compute it on real wind data for about forty sites and measure how much exposure it avoids. BACKUP: Inputs are Open-Meteo forecasts (wind, boundary-layer height, rain) and OpenStreetMap schools and hospitals plus ward population. Dispersion uses a simple Gaussian plume approximation.
